# 04 — AI literacy, champions network and change management

## Adoption principle
An AI system is not adopted simply because it is purchased or switched on. Staff need a clear permitted use case, safe boundaries, useful examples and a route to get help.

## Segmented roles
| Audience | Learning outcome | Example activity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive sponsors | Decide where AI creates value, controls and accountability | Sponsor briefing and case prioritisation | Signed success criteria |
| Department leaders | Select safe workflows and remove blockers | Workstream discovery | Approved use-case cards |
| Staff/service teams | Use approved tools, validate outputs and report issues | Scenario-based orientation | Demonstration and short knowledge check |
| Technical/data owners | Enforce access, logs, retrieval and evaluation controls | Integration/risk review | UAT/controls checklist |
| Champions | Coach peers, gather friction and escalate issues | Peer clinic | Anonymised feedback log |

## Champions network
- One nominated champion per pilot department (illustrative; dependent on staffing).
- Biweekly feedback huddle, structured prompt/use-case library, moderated FAQ.
- Champions do **not** grant tool access, approve data use, or override compliance controls.
- Log and classify friction: access issues, unclear policy, unreliable answer, duplicate process, training gap.

## Communications programme
- **Before pilot:** why this workflow, what is allowed, where to ask questions, whom to contact if wrong.
- **During pilot:** short examples, help office hours, known limitations and safe alternatives.
- **After pilot:** transparent report of what worked/failed and which features were paused.

## Adoption measurement
Track qualified activation among eligible users, sustained weekly active use, completed safe workflows, user-reported confidence, incident rates and independently checked output quality. **Do not incentivise raw prompt counts**; excessive use can signal a poor workflow.

## Resistance and inclusion
Offer non-AI path during pilots; accommodate accessibility; involve staff representatives and multilingual support as necessary; document concerns without penalising nonuse.

**No workshops or university staff were actually trained as part of this case.**
