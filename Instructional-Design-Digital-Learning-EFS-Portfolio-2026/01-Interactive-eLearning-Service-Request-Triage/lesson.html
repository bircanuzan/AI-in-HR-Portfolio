Service Request Triage

A short, decision-based eLearning experience • approx. 8 minutes

01 / Learning goals

From incoming request to accountable action

At the end, you will be able to:

  1. Distinguish urgent safety risks from routine maintenance requests.
  2. Choose a response and escalation route in a realistic scenario.
  3. Write a complete handoff note and confirm its owner.

Important: This is a hypothetical teaching scenario, not a company emergency policy. In real work, always follow your approved local safety procedures.

02 / The three-part model

Recognise → Route → Record

Recognise: Check whether there is a safety issue, service interruption or routine need; seek missing location and contact details.

Route: Safety-critical issues trigger immediate escalation according to the approved policy. Routine matters follow the normal service queue.

Record: Log exact location, observed facts (not guesses), time received, action taken, owner and confirmation of handoff.

Avoid giving a definite response timeframe unless it is listed in an approved SLA.
03 / Branching practice

Which request requires immediate escalation?

A) A meeting room needs an additional chair. B) A visitor reports sparking and smoke from a wall socket. C) A printer is out of paper.

04 / The handoff standard

Make the next person's job possible

Illustrative handoff record:

Location: 3rd floor, pantry corridor
Observed issue: occupant reports sparks and smoke from socket
Received: 09:45, 30 Oct
Action: initiated approved safety escalation; instructed reporter to follow site safety guidance
Owner: duty supervisor; receipt confirmed 09:47

Do not diagnose electrical faults or promise a repair time. Record observed facts and follow the relevant emergency procedure.
