# 05 — Resistance, Change Risks & Champions

## Different types of resistance require different interventions
People may resist because a change is unsafe, inaccessible, poorly explained, adds work, or threatens trust. Treat concerns as information—not automatically as negativity.

| Resistance / risk | Early signal | Intervention | Owner | Escalate when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Digital exclusion | Staff without device or language access | Alternate HR channel, accessible guidance, in-person help | HR Ops + IT | Employees cannot complete essential requests |
| Manager work overload | Unactioned approval queue | Capacity review, delegation clarification, manager support | HRBPs + HR Ops | SLA or delegated authority breached |
| Privacy concern | Employees reluctant to submit sensitive cases | Explain approved data routes; keep confidential human channel | Privacy + HR | Unauthorised disclosure or incorrect permissions |
| Perceived “HR is disappearing” | Increased anxiety / misinformation | Visible HR sponsor messages and human service commitments | CHRO + Comms | Engagement/trust deteriorates |
| Duplicate change initiatives | Staff report multiple simultaneous rollouts | Integrate change calendar and stagger cohorts | PMO | Readiness and service performance decline |
| Workarounds (email continues) | High share of in-scope email requests | Improve portal usability, address policy gaps and remind gently | HR Ops | Sustained channel-switching below agreed guardrail |

## Resistance log
**Fields:** risk ID, audience, actual quote (anonymised), source/date, cause hypothesis, impact, severity, proposed action, sponsor, action owner, due date, validated result, escalation path.

**Example (fictional):** R-03 — line managers report having no delegation option during leave. Root cause is likely workflow authority rather than awareness. Action: HR Ops + IT assess delegation policy, test the approval substitute and update manager guide before wider pilot.

## Champions network
- Choose champions across offices, functions, levels, desk and non-desk roles; selection by nomination and consent.
- Ask each champion to test **approved sample scenarios**, explain where support is, and share anonymised blockers.
- 30-minute weekly feedback huddle during pilot; documented issue actions.
- Champions **cannot** approve exceptions, alter access rights, make confidential HR decisions or represent the organisation without approved messaging.
- Recognise champions' effort and avoid adding unpaid workload without manager approval.

## Capacity / change-saturation review
Create a two-dimensional schedule: **affected team × concurrent initiatives**. Score each planned change's time demand, disruption, due dates and manager bandwidth on an original 1–3 scale. If the combined load reaches an agreed critical threshold, propose rescheduling, additional support, reduced pilot scope or change freeze. Threshold must be calibrated with actual workload, not invented as a universal benchmark.

## Escalation example
**Scenario:** inaccessible portal prevents a pilot employee from requesting an essential letter.
1. Offer approved alternate human service.
2. Raise accessibility incident with IT and HR service owner.
3. Record impact in risk log without storing unnecessary health/sensitive details.
4. Correct and retest with representative users.
5. Obtain owner sign-off before expansion.

**No complaints, interviews or champion sessions occurred.**
