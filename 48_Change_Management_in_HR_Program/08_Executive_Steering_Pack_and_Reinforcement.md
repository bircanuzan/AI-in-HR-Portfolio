# 08 — Executive Steering Decision Pack & 30/60/90 Reinforcement

## Sample executive summary (illustrative)
**Programme:** HR Employee Self-Service and Case Management Change

**Status: AMBER (hypothetical)** — documentation and pilot plan exist in this case simulation; the fictional leadership team would still need to verify security, policy, accessibility and manager readiness before scaling.

### What the sponsor needs to know
- **Purpose:** employees should reach HR through a clearly owned, measurable service, without losing a human or confidential channel.
- **Change complexity:** employees adopt new intake routes; managers adopt workflow approvals; HR teams adopt queue ownership and audit trail.
- **Risk:** paper readiness ≠ operational ability; portal uptake ≠ service quality.
- **Decision required:** authorise controlled pilot **only after** critical gates are evidenced by named owners.

**Decisions for sponsor** (in a real engagement):
1. Confirm executive ownership and budget/capacity.
2. Approve pilot cohort and excluded sensitive-case categories.
3. Require manager approval permissions and accessibility test evidence.
4. Confirm escalation authority and scale/hold criteria.

## Reinforcement plan
- **At +30 days:** audit case categorisation, service completion errors, access issues and manager queues. Publish common fixes and update job aids.
- **At +60 days:** compare adoption by employee access group and department; address low capability, policy confusion and repeated exceptions.
- **At +90 days:** compare against baseline with matched case types, document change fatigue, approve HR Ops ownership, then decide whether to scale.

## Hand-off / sustainment conditions
- Accountable operational owners accept issue triage, service catalogue updates and help routing.
- Communications and FAQ ownership assigned to named roles.
- Critical defects closed or isolated; security and privacy approved.
- KPI definitions, calculation logic and measurement cadence documented.
- Improvements prioritised based on actual evidence, not vanity usage counts.

## Reflective lessons — design decisions, not observed outcomes
- The **quality of the HR process** matters more than merely launching a portal.
- Resistance often reveals a genuine role, access or policy problem.
- A measured adoption programme protects accessibility and employee trust.
- Communications alone cannot solve flawed permissions, unclear SLAs or unowned exceptions.

### Competency interview example (truthful)

**Task (portfolio objective):** plan the people-side HR service transition.
**Action (completed in this case):** document the change charter, impact/RACI matrix, readiness assessment, communications, issue mitigation, 90-day plan and measurement framework.
**Result:** a completed **simulation documentation set**, not operational results or employee behaviour change.
