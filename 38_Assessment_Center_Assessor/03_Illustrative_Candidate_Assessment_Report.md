## Executive summary
Candidate A demonstrated stronger evidence of **Analytical Thinking**, especially the ability to challenge unsupported assumptions and reconcile competing records. The integrated results also suggest practical strengths in **Coaching & Development**. **Decision Making** is the clearest development priority because control/rollback ownership was inconsistent in the exercises. These observations are specific to the synthetic tasks and must not be interpreted as fixed traits or comprehensive role suitability.

## Behavioral evidence — balanced observations
**Strength 1 — Evidence checking:** In the group exercise, Candidate A asked to validate the proposed 25% handling-time improvement before presenting it as an outcome (E03). This reflects effective challenge of unverified business claims.

**Strength 2 — Development support:** In the in-basket exercise, Candidate A delegated a junior colleague's review task with a checkpoint and explicit standard (E10). This is observable coaching-related behavior within the activity.

**Improvement 1 — Governance controls:** The proposed role-play pilot lacked an explicit rollback test (E08), and the group proposal proceeded before verifying the compliance approval gate (E09). This indicates a need to include decision gates in planning exercises.

**Improvement 2 — Follow-through:** Candidate A engaged participants (E05/E06), but the group plan omitted some named owners and the developmental feedback omitted a measurable check-in (E06/E12).

## Development recommendations
1. Use a five-part decision template: goal, evidence, risk, approval owner, rollback/checkpoint.
2. Close meetings with named action owner, due date, control condition and success measure.
3. Practise a structured coaching conversation with one measurable learning goal and a follow-up review.
