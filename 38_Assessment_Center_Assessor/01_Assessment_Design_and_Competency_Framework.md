# 01 | Assessment Center Design and Competency Framework

## Fictional scenario and purpose
A fictional regional professional-services group is assessing an internal applicant for **People Operations Manager**. The assessment explores job-related competencies to inform a structured development discussion. It is **not** a hiring decision and has no validated selection cutoff.

## Four competencies and observable behavioral indicators

| Competency | Definition | Evidence of effective behavior | Development signals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Analytical Thinking | Examines data, separates facts from assumptions and reaches supported conclusions | Compares sources, tests assumptions, identifies constraints | Uses incomplete data as confirmed facts |
| Stakeholder Management | Builds understanding and manages competing operational concerns | Listens, checks priorities, negotiates next steps | Misses stakeholder needs or commitments |
| Decision Making | Prioritizes and takes accountable action while managing risk | Names owner, criteria, dependencies and fallback | Skips approval gate or rollback |
| Coaching & Development | Enables others to solve problems and improve | Asks exploratory questions, agrees follow-up, models feedback | Gives direction without goals or review |

## Exercise coverage matrix
All four competencies are observed in three exercises, rather than rated from impression alone.

| Exercise | Analytical | Stakeholder | Decision | Coaching |
| --- |:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| E1 — In-basket exercise | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| E2 — Stakeholder role-play | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| E3 — Group discussion simulation | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

## Behavioral Anchored Rating Scale — illustrative 1–5
The assessor records **behavioral evidence first** and later uses this anchored scale.

| Rating | Behavioral level |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Repeatedly misses key evidence, avoids ownership or causes an avoidable critical control failure |
| 2 | Partially addresses the problem but overlooks a significant risk, decision gate or necessary participant |
| 3 | Meets the basic role requirement with some evidence gaps or incomplete follow-up |
| 4 | Uses evidence proactively, engages the right stakeholders and specifies actions or checks |
| 5 | Consistently integrates multiple perspectives, anticipates risks and defines accountable measurable outcomes |

**Interpretation control:** A single poor behavior does not establish a stable personal trait. Compare multiple situations, record counter-evidence and document limits. Do not turn the generic 1–5 anchors into a claim of external validation.

## Assessor protocol
1. Review job purpose, competencies and exercise materials.
2. Provide participants with identical instructions and approved reasonable accommodations.
3. Observe without coaching the candidate during assessment tasks.
4. Record behavior objectively, distinguishing **what was said/done** from later interpretation.
5. Rate each indicator with a documented scale.
6. Prepare exercise-specific notes independently before panel integration.
7. Compare two assessors' observations; challenge disagreement using evidence, not hierarchy.
8. Agree scores, document unresolved ambiguity and prepare balanced feedback.
9. Store/access results only under authorized purposes, confidentiality and retention controls.
10. Invite candidate feedback about the process and improvement actions.

## Fairness and data protection
Clarify exercise relevance, permitted use, candidate consent/information, disability accommodations, language accessibility, bias controls, panel conflicts and data handling. Exclude appearance, accent, personality labels and other irrelevant inferences from scoring. No psychometric test interpretation is undertaken here.
