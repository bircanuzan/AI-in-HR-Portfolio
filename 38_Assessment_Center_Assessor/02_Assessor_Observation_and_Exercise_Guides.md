# 02 | Three Exercise Guides and Structured Behavioral Observations

## E1 — In-basket: service delivery escalation (30 minutes)
**Candidate role:** newly appointed People Operations Manager.

**Brief:** The team has 24 overdue service requests. A report says 10 are payroll-related, but a later quality review shows 3 of those belong to policy guidance. A service desk lead wants to close all cases within 48 hours. A payroll lead says two cases require signed controls before changes can be made. HR leadership asks for a concise action plan.

**Candidate tasks:** sort urgent cases, distinguish the sources, propose a 48-hour response plan, assign owners, address compliance/approval risks and identify who needs coaching.

**Assessor observation prompts:** Did the candidate challenge inconsistent counts? Identify the payroll sign-off dependency? Assign escalation owners? Propose coaching or check-ins?

**Example evidence in data:** E01, E04, E07, E10.

## E2 — Stakeholder role-play: competing priorities (20 minutes)
**Candidate role:** People Operations Manager meeting a skeptical Finance Manager.

**Role-player instructions:** Finance wants faster reconciliations but will not release a new workflow without demonstrated controls. When asked, mention that the source dataset is incomplete and past changes created rework. Do not introduce new facts inconsistently across candidates.

**Candidate tasks:** diagnose concerns, propose one realistic pilot, explain success measures, protect controls, and help a junior analyst prepare next actions.

**Assessor observation prompts:** Did the candidate check source reliability? Reflect stakeholder concerns? Identify a rollback/test gate? Create an improvement follow-up?

**Example evidence in data:** E02, E05, E08, E11.

## E3 — Group discussion: scaled HR service automation (25 minutes)
**Scenario:** a cross-functional fictional team wants to automate high-volume HR policy questions. The draft case assumes a 25% reduction in handling time; there are no independent baseline observations yet. One member is quiet; others disagree over whether to proceed.

**Candidate tasks:** examine unsupported assumptions, include quieter participants, align scope, manage human oversight and agree the next action.

**Assessor observation prompts:** Did the candidate request evidence before adopting savings claims? Engage the group? Specify a governance gate? Provide constructive developmental feedback?

**Example evidence in data:** E03, E06, E09, E12.

## Recording template
For each observed behavior capture: timestamp, exercise, what the candidate said/did (verbatim or neutral summary), competency code, evidence ID, assessor, 1–5 rating, corroboration and uncertainty.

### Example — fact versus inference
- **Observation:** "Candidate asked the payroll lead to confirm the approval owner and said the case must wait for sign-off."
- **Unsupported inference (do not record as fact):** "Candidate is naturally conscientious and always follows rules."
- **Defensible rating justification:** "Named control owner and withheld an unapproved action; consistent with Decision Making level 4 on this task."

Ratings must be **independent and evidence-linked** before calibration. The simulated A and B ratings are in [assessor_observations.csv](data/assessor_observations.csv); final consensus in [adjudicated_ratings.csv](data/adjudicated_ratings.csv).
