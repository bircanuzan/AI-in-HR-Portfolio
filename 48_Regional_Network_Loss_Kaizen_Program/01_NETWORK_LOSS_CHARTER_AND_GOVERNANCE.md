# Regional Network Loss Charter and Governance
**Fictional learning project — not a client programme**

## Hypothesis
Use a consistent network-loss control mechanism to reduce avoidable operational variance without harming fulfilment speed, availability or customer trust.

## Scope
- Regions: UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt (hypothetical).
- Operational nodes: dark stores and regional distribution centres.
- Buckets: WHL, WHD, Concessions. The original vacancy uses these labels without defining them. **Their precise employer meanings, cost attribution, inclusion/exclusion rules and data owners must be confirmed before any report is operationally valid.** Do not invent acronym expansions.
- Boundaries: losses may touch receiving, storage, scanning, order accuracy, transfers, returns, expiry/condition and concession adjustment; root cause is not assumed without evidence.
- Financial ownership: Finance owns signed loss definitions, reporting currency conversion, booking/timing controls and realized P&L benefits. Operational managers own controllable actions.

## Roles and decision rights
| Function | Proposed responsibility |
|---|---|
| Regional programme manager | Integrated plan, site prioritization, RAID, WBR/MBR cadence, executive narrative and escalation |
| Finance | Authoritative loss ledger, country comparability, approved target and actual validation |
| ICQA | Inventory variance/control investigation, adjustment and audit evidence |
| Dark-store / RDC operations | Process adoption, cycle counts where applicable, operational actions, site SOPs |
| Transportation | Chain of custody, handoffs, transit investigation and corrective controls |
| Technology / data | Trusted event flows, dashboards, data-quality alerts and access controls |
| Country leader | Site ownership, staffing/resources and risk acceptance |

## Stage gates
1. Charter — sponsor, baseline period, accountable approvers and exception rules agreed.
2. Diagnose — loss categories and root-cause confidence validated.
3. Pilot — scoped interventions with counterfactual/comparison plan.
4. Review — cost, customer impact, reversibility and sustained trend reviewed.
5. Scale — site playbook owner assigned, train-the-trainer and adoption measures.
6. BAU — operating review ownership and quarterly benefit check.

## Prioritization
Score each proposal 1–5 on materiality (validated $), customer harm, controllability, implementation effort (reverse scale), confidence of evidence and network scalability. Source and weighting require Finance and site-leader approval.

## Key risks
Misclassification between buckets; currency/inflation differences between countries; timing mismatch between invoice and adjustment; disputed ownership at handoff; data lag; cost savings shifted to other budget lines; improved financial rate but worse customer service. Track one accountable action owner, due date, evidence and escalation per risk.
