# LinkedIn case study draft

An employee asks HR for a standard letter.

Several emails, an approval chase and a spreadsheet update later, someone asks: “Where are we with this?”

That everyday friction is the starting point for my latest portfolio case study:

**Improving HR operations through process mapping, workflow automation and practical AI adoption — Microsoft Power Automate.**

Using topics from Microsoft Learn's Power Automate learning path, I designed a fictional employee-request workflow around three priorities:

**1. Map the work.**
Identify missing information, repeated handoffs, unclear ownership and approval delays.

**2. Design the workflow.**
Connect structured intake in Microsoft Forms with a SharePoint request register, HR approvals and Outlook/Teams notifications. Keep letter preparation and release under human control.

**3. Make AI adoption practical.**
Define how Copilot could help draft and explain flow logic, with HR reviewing the conditions, permissions and exception handling.

The case also includes acceptance scenarios, a monitoring plan and an illustrative business case.

At an assumed 100 requests per month, reducing active HR handling from 15 to 7 minutes would release approximately **13 hours of gross capacity monthly**, before ongoing support.

That is a modelled estimate for a fictional scenario. The project is a documented solution design; live deployment and measured results are the next validation step.

My main takeaway: HR automation starts with understanding the work, agreeing who owns each decision and deciding how success will be measured.
